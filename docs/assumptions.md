# Assumptions

| # | Assumption | Why it's reasonable | Risk if wrong | How to check |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Futures prices: FY28 $99.44/MWh, FY29 $103.47/MWh (ASX NSW base strip settles, retrieved 28 Oct 2026) | Market price at which the load can actually be hedged today | Prices move daily; results shift with the level | Hedge-price sensitivity and break-even price |
| 2 | FY30 held flat at the FY29 settle ($103.47/MWh); no FY30 quote shown on ASX on 28 Oct 2026 | Nearest quoted year; CY29 settle ($104.85) is close | FY30 cost level off by the FY29-FY30 gap | Checked on ASX Energy, 28 Oct 2026: no FY30 quote shown |
| 3 | Futures are fairly priced: re-levelled spot has the same time-weighted mean as `F` | Standard no-arbitrage starting point; makes the hedge test honest | If futures carry a risk premium or discount, the hedge has a real expected cost or saving | Realised-vs-futures runs and break-even price |
| 4 | FY22-FY24 price shapes (from 1 Oct 2021) represent FY28-FY30 shapes | Only 5-minute-settlement history available in scope | Grid mix changes (more solar, coal exits) alter shape; small number of seasons limits P95 | With/without May-July 2022; stated as a limitation |
| 5 | Load = 50 MW IT capacity × utilisation (85% weekday, 75% weekend) × intraday factor (×1.05 08:00-18:00, ×0.97 22:00-05:00) × PUE (1.20 Jun-Aug, 1.27 Mar-May and Sep-Nov, 1.35 Dec-Feb) | Informed by Uptime Institute Global Data Center Survey 2023 and IEA data centre energy analysis; cooling overhead rises in summer | Average load and seasonal shape off; hyperscale loads are often flatter | IEA (2025) *Energy and AI* is the IEA reference; the factors themselves are stylised, not taken from a table |
| 6 | Battery 20 MW / 40 MWh, 88% round-trip efficiency, capex $1,050/kW (CSIRO GenCost 2025-26, 2025 cost; final report confirms draft battery costs) | Published Australian cost benchmark | Real quotes differ; changes NPV and payback | NPV sensitivity |
| 7 | Solar profile: normalised output of one existing NSW utility-scale farm (AEMO dispatch data) | Real NSW solar shape at 5-minute resolution | One farm's outages or curtailment distort the factor | Data audit of the solar series |
| 8 | PPA strike $65/MWh energy-only (no strong source; midpoint of a $55-75/MWh commentary range that includes LGCs) | Range from published commentary; LGC-inclusive quotes overstate an energy-only strike | Expected PPA value is very sensitive to strike | Strike sensitivity $55-75 in $5 steps |
| 9 | Discount rate 8% (6-10% sensitivity) | Oxford Economics Australia (2024) for AEMO's 2026 ISP estimates an 8.0% WACC for battery storage | NPV sign could change | Sensitivity table |
| 10 | Wholesale energy cost only (no network, retail margin, loss factors, LGC/environmental charges) | These are largely the same across strategies | Total bill understated; relative comparison mostly unaffected | Stated in README and report |
| 11 | Battery: 15-year asset life, 2%/yr capacity fade (applied to annual saving), prices held at FY30 level after FY30 | Common planning life for grid batteries; fade reflects degradation | Longer or shorter life, faster fade or different post-FY30 prices change NPV | NPV sensitivity to discount rate; stated as a limitation |
| 12 | Source rule: if no solid source is found while building the model for PPA strike, discount rate or battery O&M, keep the assumption and show its sensitivity range | Avoids presenting weak sources as fact | Point estimates may look more certain than they are | Every such number shown with its range |
| 13 | PPA base case settles every interval, including negative-price intervals; sensitivity suspends settlement when spot < 0 | Both structures appear in corporate PPAs | PPA expected cost misstated if the real contract differs | Both cases reported |
| 14 | June 2022 administered and suspension prices are valid historical outcomes | They were the prices actually settled | The $300 cap truncated the tail, so crisis risk is understated | Without-May-Jul-2022 run; stated as a limitation. $300 clusters in May and Jul 2022 (145 and 151 intervals) fall outside the flagged window. Checked: no NSW administered price period outside 13-24 Jun 2022 was found; these clusters are scattered single intervals rather than a continuous $300 run, consistent with offers priced at the $300 administered cap level, so they are kept as real prices and not flagged |
| 15 | One farm's normalised output, outage days included, represents a contracted solar PPA | Real NSW solar shape and availability | Portfolio PPAs have fewer outages; PPA risk may be overstated | Stated as a limitation |
| 16 | Public holidays are treated as weekdays in the load profile | Data centre load runs on IT demand, not the office calendar | Holiday load slightly overstated if utilisation does dip | Small share of days; not tested |
| 17 | Time-of-day bands and all timestamps use AEMO market time (AEST, no daylight saving) | Matches price timestamps exactly and needs no timezone conversion | During daylight saving the load bands sit one hour off the site's local clock | Effect on cost judged small; stated as a limitation |
| 18 | Battery fixed O&M 2.5% of capex per year ($26.25/kW/yr), sensitivity 1.5-3.5% | No citable GenCost O&M value found within the time box; kept per the source rule (#12) | NPV and equivalent annual cost shift | O&M rows in the NPV table |
| 19 | Market price cap $23,200/MWh for FY28-FY30 (AEMC 2026-27 value); floor -$1,000/MWh | Latest published cap; later years are CPI-indexed and not yet set | Cap only binds if re-levelled prices reach it, which scaling down makes rare | Count of clipped intervals in `relevel_check.csv` |
| 20 | Battery in service from 1 July 2027, so FY28 is year 1 of fade (FY29 ×0.98, FY30 ×0.98²) | Lines up the NPV years with the forward years | Later start shifts fade by a year | Small effect; not tested |
| 21 | Trading date = calendar date (00:00-24:00 market time) for daily battery cycles and bootstrap days | Simple and consistent with the calendar staging | Days split a few hours differently from a 04:00-04:00 market day | Not tested; stated as a limitation |
| 22 | Battery thresholds use a trailing 7-day window that can straddle two historical FYs with different scale factors | Prices are re-levelled before dispatch; the effect is limited to the first week of each FY | Slightly different dispatch in those weeks | Stated as a limitation |
| 23 | In the run without May-Jul 2022, every simulated July comes from July 2023 and the battery idles for a week after the gap | Only July 2022 and July 2023 exist in the pool | Less variety in simulated Julys for that sensitivity | Stated as a limitation |
| 24 | Wrap-around blocks join a month's last days to its first days, which are not consecutive in time | Gives every day equal weight; affects at most one block per month | A spike event at month end can be cut | Accepted trade-off for unbiased sampling |
| 25 | The PPA suspends settlement only when spot is below zero; intervals at exactly $0 still settle | Matches the brief and the usual "negative price" clause | Contracts that suspend at zero or below would raise the suspended break-even strike above $66.99 | Count of intervals at exactly $0 is about 1,900; a zero-or-below variant could be added |
| 26 | The 100% hedge is sized on the pool's average load (53.06 MW); the simulated average is about 52.9 MW | Simple, and the same MW is used in every run | The hedge is about 0.4% over the simulated load; payoff per $1/MWh is $0.466M as reported | Size from simulated annual load if tighter accuracy is needed |
| 27 | Expected cost is the exact expectation from `expected_draws` weights; P95 is simulated and carries sampling noise (seed SD $0.13M for spot FY28) | Costs are linear in the daily components, so no simulation is needed for the mean | P95 differences under about $0.3M between strategies could be noise | Seed check in the validation log |
